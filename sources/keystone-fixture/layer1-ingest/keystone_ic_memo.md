# Project Keystone - Final Investment Committee Memorandum

## Decision requested

Approve the acquisition of Alderstone at **$108.0m** of enterprise value, funded with **$62.0m** of sponsor cash equity, **$12.0m** of seller rollover and **$42.8m** of first-lien opening debt.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Investment thesis

1. Scaled platform in outsourced technical compliance with long-standing customer relationships.
2. Historical growth and improving margins, but seller EBITDA must be normalized.
3. Organic growth through pricing, customer expansion and new logos.
4. Moderate, gradual margin improvement from utilization and project discipline.
5. Fragmented add-on landscape, excluded from the standalone base case.
6. Return construction based on operations and deleveraging, not multiple expansion.

## Concern block / what must be true

1. Riverton remains sufficiently durable that a material reduction is a downside, not the base case.
2. The Firm prices the transaction on **$11.4m**, not seller or covenant EBITDA.
3. Organic revenue can grow without relying on unidentified acquisitions.
4. Margin expands gradually without aggressive add-backs.
5. The **$2.0m** integration program is funded and governed.
6. Billing, WIP and DSO remain manageable through systems consolidation.
7. The capital structure remains solvent under customer or integration downside.
8. The investment works at a **9.0x** exit multiple.

## Risk debate - customer concentration

Challenge: The seller's **7.6%** largest-account figure masks an **18.2%** ultimate-parent relationship. Riverton has no minimum-volume commitment and ordinary termination rights.

Deal-team response: The relationship spans approximately nine years, six facilities and multiple service lines. The formal downside case assumes a material customer reduction.

Committee judgement: The risk is material and accepted only on the ultimate-parent basis, with monthly reporting, a pre-closing reference call and change-of-control notice.

## Risk debate - integration and execution

Challenge: Alderstone has acquired four companies without fully integrating billing, project accounting and customer data. A migration failure could affect EBITDA and cash, not merely back-office efficiency.

Deal-team response: The remaining program is budgeted at **$2.0m**, a dedicated integration leader will be appointed, major cutovers require board approval and a **$4.0m** board-designated integration and liquidity capacity will be preserved.

Committee judgement: The integration risk is acceptable with governance, but the central risk is customer disruption and working-capital absorption resulting from execution failure.

## Combined-risk debate

The dissenting member argues that the customer and integration risks are correlated and should be modeled together. The majority accepts the risk with conditions, concluding that simultaneous severe realization is possible but not the base case.

## IC conditions

1. No increase in EV above **$108.0m**.
2. Purchase price based on **$11.4m** of firm EBITDA.
3. Sponsor initial cash equity of at least **$62.0m**.
4. First-lien debt no greater than **$42.8m**.
5. Opening cash at least **$3.0m** and revolver undrawn.
6. Riverton reference call and change-of-control notice completed without adverse response.
7. Normalized NWC target of **$8.4m**; unsupported aged WIP excluded.
8. Market-rate founder-related headquarters lease executed.
9. Independent chair appointed and integration leader hired within 60 days.
10. **$4.0m** board-designated integration and liquidity capacity preserved.
11. Major systems cutovers require independent readiness review and fallback plan.
12. First add-on requires separate board approval; second add-on requires two clean operating quarters.
13. Monthly ultimate-parent, WIP, DSO, utilization, debt and covenant reporting.

## Recorded dissent

One member votes against the investment because the **18.2%** customer, incomplete integration and **9.5x** firm entry multiple do not provide sufficient compensation for correlated risk. The dissenting member would require a lower price, customer-retention holdback, contractual volume protection or a period of demonstrated systems stability.
