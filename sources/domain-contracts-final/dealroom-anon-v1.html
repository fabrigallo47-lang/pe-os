Investment Implications
Layer Assessment
Where does value accrue in this stack?
The most defensible value accrues to companies with recurring revenue, proprietary data, mission-critical control points or irreplicable heritage. Pure hardware manufacturing carries thin margins at scale. Astrelia sits between the two: manufacturing today, recurring and data optionality over time. The investment case depends on how much of that services migration is underwritten at entry valuation.
Astrelia Positioning
Where does Astrelia play, and what is the competitive moat?
Astrelia spans Layers 3, 4 and 5, with optionality in Layers 6 and 7. Its moat is deep-space flight heritage, vertical integration toward 80% in-house production and defense-grade mission reliability. These are difficult to replicate quickly and create genuine procurement barriers in institutional and defense contracts.
Valuation Anchor
How should Astrelia be valued today?
Astrelia should be valued primarily as a vertically integrated smallsat platform, subsystem and mission-operations company — consistent with comparable transactions at 5.5 to 6.5x forward revenue. NebulaOS and data services represent a separately underwritten upside option, not a primary base-case input, pending confirmation of data rights, customer contracts and revenue visibility.

What is Astrelia?

Every satellite consists of two functional components: the platform (also called the “bus”), the structural chassis, power generation and storage system, on-board computers, and the Attitude Determination and Control System (ADCS) that keeps the spacecraft precisely pointed in orbit,and the payload, the instrument the satellite carries to perform its mission. A payload might be an electro-optical camera for Earth imaging, a Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR,a radar system that generates high-resolution images through cloud cover and in darkness), or a communications array. Astrelia designs and manufactures the platform, and produces the majority of the critical electronics within it in-house.

Founded in Northern Italy in 2008 by Davide Renna, founder and Chief Executive Officer, Astrelia has grown from a specialist engineering consultancy to a full-spectrum satellite manufacturer with 245 employees. Its primary facility, StarYard, covers 12,000 square metres. The company also operates offices in Huntsville, Alabama (US manufacturing footprint), Toulouse (ESA proximity), and Denver, Colorado (payload laboratory, opening 2025).

Astrelia's model is evolving from one-time satellite manufacturing toward a broader space infrastructure platform: selling spacecraft, monetizing proprietary subsystems, operating satellites through mission life and developing a data-services layer through NebulaOS.

Verified Qualification Track Record
Environmental Qualification Campaign
2022  ·  THIRD-PARTY TEST FACILITY
Across 2022, Astrelia ran a full environmental qualification campaign on the ORION Plus bus at an independent third-party test facility, covering thermal-vacuum cycling, random vibration and EMC/EMI to prime-contractor acceptance thresholds. The bus passed on first pass across all test categories, a result the company has used to shorten qualification review cycles with new prime-contractor customers, since re-qualification is normally the largest single delay item in a first program with a new manufacturer.
Serial Delivery Record
2019–2025  ·  14 PROGRAMS
Astrelia has delivered 14 programs since 2019 across defense, Earth Observation and scientific customers with zero critical-anomaly deliveries and no schedule slip greater than four weeks against contracted delivery dates. Two deliveries required post-delivery corrective action on ground-segment interfaces, both resolved within the warranty period at no cost to the customer. Full delivery log and corrective-action register available in the data room.

Additional programs include two VELA satellites, the STORMCube atmospheric monitor, and several classified defence payloads. Full delivery manifest available in the data room.

Technological Differentiation

Astrelia's technological differentiation is anchored in four areas: qualification record, vertical integration, modular dual-use architecture and delivery reliability.

First, Astrelia has run its qualification campaigns through independent third-party test facilities rather than self-certifying, and has a 14-program serial delivery record with zero critical-anomaly deliveries. This is strategically relevant because prime contractors treat qualification and delivery history as the primary substitute for their own in-house testing; a clean record shortens the supplier-approval cycle on new programs, which is otherwise the largest source of schedule risk for a new manufacturer relationship.

Second, Astrelia is not only a satellite integrator. The company has built a growing proprietary subsystem stack, including on-board computer and data handling, power control and distribution, battery systems and software-defined radio, while targeting 80%+ in-house component production. This vertical integration should improve control over supply chain, qualification, cost, schedule and reliability, while enabling reuse of core technologies across multiple platform variants.

Third, the ORION platform family is designed around a modular, dual-use architecture serving defense, Earth Observation (EO) and scientific missions. Reuse of core platform elements across multiple end-markets should reduce non-recurring engineering requirements, support faster delivery timelines and improve manufacturing learning curves, while allowing mission-specific tailoring of payloads, software and operating requirements.

Finally, Astrelia reports zero critical-anomaly deliveries across its 14-program base to date. If confirmed in technical diligence, this reliability record would represent a material procurement advantage in institutional and defense programs, where qualification history, supplier trust and delivery continuity are central award criteria.
